**Kalaris Labs is an independent AI research lab, founded in India in 2026, building the infrastructure for the agentic
era.** Software is learning to act: agents now write code, transact, negotiate and coordinate for people and
institutions, faster than anything beneath them can adapt. Everyone is building the agents. We are building what they
will stand on.

<Note>
  **Our motto: the rules have to exist before the actors do.** The internet, the web and finance all got their
  infrastructure late, after the damage. When software acts at machine speed, this era does not get to wait.
</Note>

## Why the lab exists

The crowded bet is that more capable models will fix the problems of autonomous agents on their own. We think that bet
is wrong. Scaling makes models more capable; it does not make them accountable. Capability without accountability is
not progress. It is a liability with a network connection.

Agents are entering codebases, markets and workflows without clear mandates. They talk to one another without shared
protocols. They are graded by benchmarks they can learn to game. When many of them meet in the same system, they
behave in ways no single evaluation was built to predict. Kalaris Labs exists to close that gap.

## What we build

| Layer | What it is | Where it is today |
|---|---|---|
| Harnesses | The environments, tools and feedback loops that turn a model into an agent, and let it learn from its own work | [Kalki](/kalki), [Agent Kalki](/research/papers/agent-kalki), [The Harness Is the Lab](/research/papers/the-harness-is-the-lab) |
| Protocols | Shared ways for agents to identify themselves, communicate, delegate and prove what they did | [AMARA](/amara), [Agent Coupling Protocol](/research/papers/agent-coupling-protocol), [LocalLink](/tools/locallink) |
| Markets and mandates | Explicit, checkable statements of who an agent works for and what it may spend | [Agent economies series](/research/agent-economies) |
| Simulations | Worlds that stress agents before they are trusted with real ones | [Agent economies series](/research/agent-economies); Agents on Web (planned, work begins 2027) |
| Evaluations | Benchmarks that are hard to game, reported with their failures as plainly as their successes | [PrincipalBench v2](/benchmarks/principalbench), [HiddenLaw](/benchmarks/hiddenlaw), [evaluation principles](/benchmarks/evaluation-principles) |
| Tools for science and security | Open skills that help agents do careful science, and a scanner that checks those skills before an agent trusts them | [Research Agent Skills](/research-agent-skills), [Skill Doctor](/skill-doctor) |

We also publish on efficient inference ([Anudhi](/research/papers/anudhi), [Pipit](/research/papers/pipit),
[Banyan](/research/papers/banyan)) and computational neuroscience
([fly brain resuscitation](/research/papers/fly-brain-resuscitation)). See [all papers](/research).

## Principles

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Safety is a design constraint, not a department" icon="shield-check" defaultOpen>
    An agent should do what it was asked, within the limits it was given, and nothing more. Its actions should be
    traceable, its incentives readable, and its results verifiable by someone other than itself.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="We build in the open" icon="git-branch">
    Infrastructure that only one company can read is not infrastructure; it is a toll booth. Our papers are open
    access and our code is on [GitHub](https://github.com/KalarisLabs) under open-source licenses.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="We build from India, for everyone" icon="globe">
    India has shown what happens when a foundational layer is treated as a public good: a payments network that
    hundreds of millions of people use every day and that any bank or app can build on. The agentic era deserves
    rails like that.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="We report what we have not measured" icon="scale">
    Every paper page states its evidence level: proposal, partial implementation, simulation, experiment or position.
    We state limitations next to results and withdraw results that do not hold up, as with the
    [PrincipalBench v1 rankings](/research/papers/principalbench-v2).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="We measure ourselves in lives touched" icon="heart-handshake">
    Revenue matters because it keeps the work going; impact is the point.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
